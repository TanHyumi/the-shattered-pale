    The Shattered Pale (Wiki Home)¶

    Overview¶

    Welcome to The Shattered Pale, a diverse and expansive world shaped by grand deities of a forgotten era. After a ruinous and divisive war, the Celestials, Dragons, and Primordials were banished away, incapable of fully returning to their creations.

    Now, in the distant future, the people of the world are still recovering from their absence. Divine power channeled by the devout trickles through the imperfect veil, arcanists wrestle with the chaos of the magical weave, and fractured alliances and rising factions race to uncover ancient artifacts or otherwise strengthen their influence.

    Themes¶

    Myth and Legend¶

    • Superstitions, folktales, and rumors are commonplace. While many are pure fiction, shaped by the great storytellers of past generations, some may be worth believing in.

    Exploration and Adventure¶

    • The war-torn world is full of abandoned ruins, forgotten relics, and bountiful caches. Many trailblazers and opportunists alike find the allure of adventure, and profit, too great to ignore.

    Dangerous and Rare Magic¶

    • For a time, powerful gods used magic to shape the world and its people, before passing it down to their students. Today, magic practitioners are rare and their spells often unstable.

    Political Intrigue¶

    • In the dominions of court and council or crime and corruption, only those strong enough can thrive. Play your cards right and your choices can shift alliances, shape nations, and end (or begin) wars.

    Houserules¶

    Resting¶

    • Short rests require the party to take at least 8 hours of downtime, usually at the end of a day, during which they can perform only light activity. If this downtime is interrupted by more than a half hour of heavy activity (fighting, spellcasting, traveling, etc), the character gains no benefit from the rest.

    • Long rests require the party to take at least 48 hours of downtime in a safe area, during which they can perform normal downtime activities such as crafting, carousing, researching, gambling, shopping, light training, etc.

    Death Saves¶

    • If a player needs to roll a Death Save, it is to be done in private -- neither the DM nor the other players should see or know the outcome.

    • Whenever you are making Death Saves and either Stabilize or heal above 0 HP, you receive 1 level of Exhaustion. You cannot exceed 5 levels of Exhaustion in this way.

    • Last Stand - Once per Long Rest, you can choose to use the Last Stand ability before rolling a Death Save. Doing so allows you to take an Action and move up to half your speed (in either order). During the Last Stand, you cannot regain any hit points or receive any temporary hit points. After the Last Stand ends, you automatically receive 2 failed Death Saves and must proceed to roll another Death Save before ending your turn.

    Combat¶

    • Tactical Initiative Shift - After initiative is rolled, the party may collectively decide whether half of the group (rounded down) will relinquish their rolled initiative scores. These characters immediately move to act directly before or after a chosen ally in the turn order. The characters choosing to move can only move down in initiative. This decision must be made before any creature takes its first turn in combat.

    • Desperate Casting - Once per short rest, a character with the Spellcasting feature may strain their magical reserves to regain a spell slot. As a bonus action, they recover one expended spell slot of their choice, up to 4th level, and immediately gain a number of levels of exhaustion equal to the level of the recovered spell. This ability cannot restore spell slots beyond the highest level the character can normally cast.

    Character Creation¶

    • At level 1, choose one skill in which you have proficiency. You gain expertise with that skill, which means your proficiency bonus is doubled for any ability check you make with it.

    Skill Challenges¶

    Skill Challenges measure a group’s ability to work together to execute a complex plan, survive a hazardous environment, or overcome a multi-stage obstacle.

    To complete a Skill Challenge, the party must accumulate a target number of successes before reaching a specific threshold of failures (for example, 5 successes before 3 failures to escape a collapsing two-story dungeon, or 7 successes before 3 failures for a larger three-story dungeon). Players act in turns, with each turn allowing a player to make a check or expend a resource toward the party's goal.

    • Rules of Engagement:

      • Proficiency & Non-Proficient Checks: Players are encouraged to rely on their heroic archetypes by utilizing skills in which they have proficiency. A player may attempt a check using a skill in which they lack proficiency, but doing so significantly increases the Difficulty Class (DC) of the check.

      • Per-Player Skill Limits: An individual character cannot use the same skill more than once during a single Skill Challenge. However, different characters may use the same skill (for example, two different players may both attempt an Athletics check during separate turns).

      • Expending Resources: A player may sacrifice a spell slot or a limited-use feature (such as Channel Divinity or a Superiority Die) to contribute to the challenge. Depending on the resource used and its relevance to the situation, the DM determines whether the effort grants Advantage on the check or bypasses the check entirely to award an automatic success.

      • Consequences of Failure: Reaching the failure threshold does not always result in a Total Party Wipe or complete failure. Instead, it often introduces significant setbacks, lingering complications, lost time/resources, or immediate new hazards that the party must now face.
